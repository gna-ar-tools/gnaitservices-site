It rarely starts with a hacker. It starts with an email that looks normal.

Most small-business losses don't come from movie-style hacking. They come from a message that looks like it's from someone you trust, sent at a busy moment.

"We changed banks."

A vendor you pay every month emails new bank details. The email looks right. It's actually from a look-alike address, or from the vendor's real mailbox after it was broken into. You pay the next invoice, and the payment goes to the wrong account.

"I need this wired today. Keep it quiet."

A message from "the owner" or a partner asks for an urgent payment or gift cards while they're "in a meeting." The name is right. The sender isn't.

"This is your bank's fraud department."

A caller says your account is at risk and asks you to read back a code or move money to a "safe" account. Banks don't ask for that.

The good news: most of these scams are stopped by a few simple habits and settings. That's what we help you put in place.
