Before You Pay a Changed Bank Account: 6 Checks That Stop Fake Vendor Payments

A vendor emails to say they've "switched banks." Sometimes it's true. Often it's a scammer who has copied or hacked the vendor's email. Once the money is sent, it's very hard to get back. Do these checks every time, before any money moves.

Download the PDF
  1. Stop and hold the payment. Don't pay the new account, and don't reply to the email yet. A real vendor won't mind waiting a day.
  2. Check the sender's address closely. Compare it letter by letter with the one you have on file. billing@acme-supp1y.com is not billing@acme-supply.com.
  3. Watch for pressure. Messages like "Pay today or the job stops" or "Don't call, just reply here" are warning signs.
  4. Call the vendor on a number you already have. Use your records or their official website, never a number from the email. Ask them to confirm the new bank details out loud.
  5. Write it down. Note who you spoke to, the date and the time. For large or unusual amounts, have a second person approve the change.
  6. Make it a rule for everyone. No bank-detail change without a call-back, and that includes part-timers and anyone filling in. If a payment already looks wrong, call your bank right away, because speed matters.

If any check fails, don't pay.
